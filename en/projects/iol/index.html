Designing workplaces for the fintech world involves the challenge of translating a complex digital ecosystem into architecture: speed and technological innovation coexisting with solidity and institutional backing. In the case of IOL (Invertir Online), one of the sector's unicorn companies and a key part of Grupo Supervielle, the project called for a tectonic identity that would reflect its leadership and dynamism. With this conceptual premise, we developed its new offices on the 23rd floor of the QIUB building in Palermo.

Working on the 23rd floor, with an open plan exposed to panoramic views, our first design decision was not to fragment the urban landscape. We designed a functional capsule: a compact central core that concentrates and contains all the company's fixed, technical and service functions, including coffee point, pantry, toilets, copy point, storage areas, booths and data centre. This geometric operation acts as the heart of the floor, freeing the building's perimeter. As a result, every occupied workstation faces the glazed facade, ensuring full natural light and democratising views of the city for the entire team.

The perimeter around this central capsule is organised as a fluid space, avoiding the monotony of a conventional large open-plan office. The programme is divided according to specific patterns of use that respond to contemporary working methods: operational areas designed for maximum acoustic and lighting comfort, helping staff to focus while giving every position an outward view; collaborative areas that encourage spontaneous exchange, group synergy and meetings of different kinds, whether formal or relaxed; and a breakout zone strategically placed at the end of the floor's radial circulation, conceived as an informal setting supported by the capsule's fixed functions.

The material proposal seeks a restrained and warm expression that conveys IOL's solidity as a company. Bespoke furniture and lighting design play leading roles in shaping the atmosphere of the office. The contrast between the tectonic opacity of the central service block and the transparent permeability of the operational areas creates a continuous visual balance. The result is a space that not only meets the client's demanding technical and operational requirements, but also establishes itself as an efficient, flexible and transparent work of corporate architecture.
