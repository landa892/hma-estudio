In our proposal for the Argentine Pavilion at the 2025 Venice Architecture Biennale, we present a circuit identifying six types of intelligence that have shaped human action throughout our evolution:

individual, collective, natural, artificial, emotional, and rational.

While each intelligence operates individually, they interact with one another in diverse ways. This interaction between any two forms of intelligence generates a tension—a state of chaos—manifested through the seemingly illogical expressiveness of the structures supporting them.

From these points of conflict emerges a synapse—an interconnection or relationship between parts—creating a structure that, amidst the chaos, seeks to interlink (in the very sense of *intelligence*) the intelligences in question, while serving as the physical support for the exhibition content. Thus, the visitor traverses the pavilion by weaving together these six types of intelligence, creating five intersections; ultimately, it is these five focal points that display, support, and illuminate the work as a whole.

These five walkable support structures—realized through scaffolding and articulated joints—are arranged within the space in a seemingly random pattern. They contrast with a central, serene, linear path that invites visitors to pause, sit beneath the "cloud" of these various intelligences, and rediscover the capacity for contemplation.

The content is organized across the five intersections according to a chronological curatorial approach. The exhibition strategy for each intersection presents a selection of works that share an integral, direct relationship with the subject matter, following a timeline that begins with individual intelligence and culminates in artificial intelligence.

MATERIALS AND THEIR SIGNIFICANCE

The pavilion rises from a combination and arrangement of tubular components and joints forming scaffolding structures—representing humanity's capacity for intelligent evolution—where solid wooden planks provide the framework for displaying audiovisual content. Simultaneously, the scaffolding supports and elevates six suspended volumes that appear to levitate within the space, creating the project's circulation path. These lightweight volumes crowning the structures are fashioned from ethereal white fabric, created by repurposing discarded plastic-woven bulk bags (often known as "big bags"). This reinforced material is typically used to transport sand during the hydraulic fracturing (fracking) process for oil and natural gas extraction in unconventional fields—specifically, the Vaca Muerta formation. It is a material designed for single use followed by incineration. Reusing it in this project aims to highlight the need for ecological awareness, foster circular micro-economies driven by entrepreneurs who reclaim waste materials for secondary uses (*1), and promote a vision of a future defined by energy self-sufficiency and its positive implications for Argentina.

Following a similar rationale and objective, the project's central walkway is formed from a roll of vinyl rubber produced by recycling discarded plastics (*2). Cast aluminum benches will be placed along this linear path, showcasing the work of young Argentine designers (*3).
